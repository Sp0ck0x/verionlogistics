The deliverable

What lands in your inbox

Every job ends the same way — a short written report you can act on without us. Here's how one is laid out.

01
Summary

Our answer in one paragraph, at the top. What we found, what we'd do about it, and roughly what it's worth. If you read nothing else, you read this.

02
What you're paying now

Your current costs set out line by line, from the documents you sent — so you can check we've read them correctly before trusting anything that follows.

03
Findings

Each issue with the amount attached and the evidence behind it: the comparison quotes we obtained, the tariff line we checked, the clause in your terms. Nothing asserted without a source.

04
Options

Usually three, costed and compared, with the trade-off named for each — including the one we recommend. We tell you what it costs you, not only what it saves.

05
Who to contact

Named carriers or forwarders with their quotes and contact details. You approach them directly; we're not in the chain and take nothing from them.

06
What we couldn't check

Anything we couldn't verify with the information available, stated plainly so you know the limits of the answer.

Reports run to a few pages, not fifty. Ask and we'll send a fully worked example before you commit to anything.

Services

Five things we do

Each is a defined piece of work with a fixed price and a written result.

01

Quote check

You've been quoted for a shipment and want to know whether the price is reasonable. We compare it against the market and tell you plainly whether it holds up.

Best first step. Cheapest way to find out where you stand.

02

Import cost review

Everything you currently pay to import — freight, duty, clearance charges, contract terms — worked through and listed where it looks wrong or expensive, with the amounts attached.

For regular importers. Most useful if you ship the same goods repeatedly.

03

Route and carrier sourcing

For a specific shipment: the sensible route options, what each should cost, and carriers who can actually do it within your budget. You get their quotes and contact details.

For new lanes. New supplier, new country, or a shipment you've not made before.

04

HS code and incoterms check

A short, focused review of how your goods are classified for customs and which trade terms you buy on. Both are common sources of quiet, repeated overpayment.

Smallest job. Often finds the single largest error.

05

Project and oversized cargo planning

For heavy or out-of-gauge shipments: whether the route works, what permits and paperwork are needed, roughly what it should cost, and whether the operator quotes you have are reasonable.

Planning only. We assess before you commit; we don't run the move.

Questions

Before you get in touch

Are you a freight forwarder or customs broker?
No. We don't move cargo and we don't file customs declarations — your own forwarder and broker keep doing that. We're the outside opinion on what they're charging you and whether the setup makes sense.
Why don't you publish prices?
Because a rate card would be wrong for most people who read it. Checking one quote on one lane and reviewing a year of imports across six product lines are very different jobs, and pricing them the same would mean overcharging the first to cover the second. Send us what you're asking about and you'll have a fixed figure within a working day, free and with no obligation.
What if you don't find anything?
Then we tell you that, in writing, with the comparisons that led us there. Confirming you're already paying a fair price is a useful answer, and it's the one we'd rather give than invent a problem.
Will my forwarder find out?
Not from us. We work from the documents you send and gather comparison quotes independently. What you do with the report afterwards is entirely your decision.
Do you work with small importers and online sellers?
Yes — including Amazon and direct-to-consumer sellers importing from Asia, where there's usually no in-house logistics person to check anything. A small shipment carries the same classification and contract-term mistakes as a large one, and they repeat on every order.
What do you need from me to start?
Whatever you already have: a quote, a recent invoice, a packing list, or just a description of the goods and the route. If something's missing we'll tell you what else we need before quoting.
Who are we?
Verion Logistics SIA is a Latvian company, registered in 2024 and VAT-registered in the EU under LV40203552228 — you can verify both on the Latvian business register and in the EU VIES system.
